# Freelancing — First Client to Stable Income

## Phase 1: Offer Design and Foundation (Weeks 1–4)
Freelancing fails most often from offering too many services to too many people. Niching down feels limiting but increases revenue. Generalists compete on price. Specialists command premiums.

Niche definition (the three-circle method):
- Circle 1: Skills you have (or can build quickly)
- Circle 2: Problems a specific industry/audience has
- Circle 3: Services that command real money
- Your niche is where all three overlap

Offer design rules:
- Solve one specific problem for one specific type of client
- Package it: hourly billing creates scope creep; project pricing creates profit
- Price on value delivered, not hours worked
- Minimum viable offer: one deliverable, one price, one delivery timeline

Portfolio if you have no clients yet:
- Spec work: build 2-3 samples as if you had a real client (pick a fake brand, solve their real problem)
- Pro bono: one project for a nonprofit or friend's business at no cost, with permission to publish
- Personal project: your own website, your own brand, your own published work

Milestone: Offer written as a one-pager (what you do, who it's for, what they get, what it costs, how it works).

## Phase 2: First Client Acquisition (Weeks 5–10)
Most first freelance clients come from warm network, not cold outreach. Start there.

Warm outreach sequence:
- Week 5: Message 20 people you know — previous colleagues, classmates, friends in business
- Message template: "I've started freelancing as a [specific role]. I'm looking for my first paid project — do you know anyone who might need [specific service]? Even a referral would be amazing."
- Don't sell directly to warm contacts — ask for referrals

Cold outreach (after warm network is exhausted):
- Identify 10 businesses that could benefit from your service but clearly don't have it
- Research each one (2 hours) before reaching out
- Send a personalized, specific message: "I noticed [specific thing on their website/social]. I've helped similar businesses with [specific result]. Would you be open to a 20-minute conversation?"
- Volume required: 20 cold messages to produce 1-2 responses at typical rates

Freelance platforms (as supplemental, not primary):
- Upwork, Fiverr — high competition, price pressure, race to bottom
- Better: niche communities (Slack groups, Discord, subreddits, industry forums) where clients congregate

Discovery call structure:
- 30 min maximum
- First 15 min: ask about their problem, goals, past attempts, budget range
- Last 15 min: explain how you'd approach it, next steps
- Never pitch during the discovery call — gather information, send the proposal after

Milestone: First paid project signed. Even $200. The psychological threshold is everything.

## Phase 3: Sustainable Freelance Business (Weeks 11–24)
The goal of Phase 3 is systems that replace hustle with consistency.

Client retention is cheaper than acquisition:
- Every client relationship ends with: "Who else do you know who might need this?"
- Send a check-in email 30 days after project delivery
- Offer a monthly retainer for ongoing work — recurring revenue stabilizes income

Pricing progression:
- Raise prices with each new client (or every 90 days)
- Track: How many proposals convert? If >50%, prices are too low.
- If <10% convert, prices may be too high or offer not clear enough

Referral system:
- Ask every satisfied client for a referral (most won't volunteer — you must ask)
- Offer a referral fee or thank-you gift
- One satisfied client = 3-5 potential new clients through their network

Systems to build:
- Contract template (use AND.CO or Bonsai for legal protection)
- Invoice template with payment terms (net-7 or net-14, not net-30)
- Scope document: written confirmation of what's included and what isn't
- Offboarding checklist: deliver files, request testimonial, ask for referral

Income targets:
- Month 1-2: First 1-2 clients, $500-2000 total
- Month 3-4: 2-4 active clients, $2000-5000/month
- Month 5-6: Selective intake, raise rates 20%, build waiting list

Milestone: Monthly income from freelancing equals 1-2 weeks of previous employment income.

## Common Pitfalls
- Underpricing out of desperation: Cheap clients create the most problems and referrals to cheap clients
- No contract: Every freelancer learns this lesson once via a non-paying client
- Taking every project: Saying yes to bad-fit projects blocks capacity for good ones
- Inconsistent outreach: Feast-famine cycles come from stopping outreach during busy periods

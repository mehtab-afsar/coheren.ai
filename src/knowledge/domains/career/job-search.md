# Job Search — Structured Approach to Landing Interviews

## Phase 1: Foundation and Positioning (Weeks 1–3)
Most job searches fail because of positioning, not effort. Sending 100 generic applications produces worse results than sending 20 highly targeted ones. Fix positioning first.

Target role definition (Day 1–3):
- Define your target: specific job title, industry sector, company size, location/remote
- Find 20 job listings that match — read every word of each one
- Extract the 15 most common skills and requirements across all 20 listings
- These 15 skills become your positioning anchors

Resume audit checklist:
- ATS-compatible format: no tables, no columns, no images, no headers/footers
- 1 page for under 10 years experience, 2 pages maximum ever
- Each bullet: "Accomplished X by doing Y which resulted in Z" (the XYZ formula)
- Quantify everything: "managed a team" → "managed 8-person team delivering 3 products per quarter"
- Remove: objective statement, references available, high school education, skills everyone has (Word, email)
- Keywords: Use exact phrases from job listings — ATS matches keywords literally

LinkedIn profile audit:
- Headline: "[Job Title] | [Key Skill] | [Key Skill] | [Industry]" — not "Looking for opportunities"
- About section: 3 paragraphs — who you are, what you do well, what you're looking for
- Experience: match your resume, plus add context about impact
- Skills: reorder to put target skills first — they affect search ranking
- Connections: 500+ connections significantly improves profile visibility

Milestone: Resume and LinkedIn profile reviewed by 2 people in your target field. All XYZ bullets applied.

## Phase 2: Active Application and Networking (Weeks 4–10)
Application math: average job search produces an interview from 10-20% of applications. Target 10 quality applications per week minimum.

Application system:
- Track every application in a spreadsheet: company, role, date applied, source, status, follow-up date
- Set follow-up reminders: if no response in 10 business days, send one polite follow-up
- Do not apply to more jobs than you can track and follow up on

Networking (produces 70-80% of hires):
- Connect with 5 new people per week in your target field on LinkedIn
- Message template: "Hi [Name], I'm transitioning into [role] and your work at [company] caught my eye. Would you have 15 minutes for a virtual coffee to share how you got started? No pressure if not."
- Goal: 2 informational interviews per week
- Informational interviews ask for advice, not jobs — they often lead to referrals

Job posting sources by priority:
1. Referrals: 4x more likely to get interviews
2. Company career pages (not aggregators)
3. LinkedIn Jobs
4. Indeed, Glassdoor (higher competition, lower signal)
5. Niche job boards for your industry

Milestone: 2 informational interviews completed, 20 applications sent, 2 positive responses (phone screen or interview).

## Phase 3: Interview Execution (Weeks 11–16)
Most candidates fail interviews not from lack of knowledge but from lack of preparation structure.

The STAR framework for behavioral questions:
- Situation: Set the context (1-2 sentences)
- Task: What you were responsible for (1 sentence)
- Action: What you specifically did (3-4 sentences — this is the meat)
- Result: Measurable outcome (1-2 sentences)

Prepare 10 STAR stories before any interview. Each story should be adaptable to multiple questions. Common questions: "Tell me about a time you failed", "Describe a conflict", "Your greatest achievement", "When you went above and beyond."

Technical interview preparation (if applicable):
- Practice on Glassdoor reviews for that specific company's interview process
- Leetcode Easy × 30, Medium × 20 before technical screens
- System design: review common patterns (load balancing, caching, database sharding)

Negotiation:
- Never accept the first offer on the spot — "I need 48 hours to review it" is always appropriate
- Research: Levels.fyi, Glassdoor, LinkedIn Salary, Blind for compensation data
- Counter-offer: Start 15-20% above the offer — they expect negotiation
- Negotiate everything: salary, equity, signing bonus, PTO, remote work, start date

Milestone: First offer received. Negotiation attempted regardless of outcome.

## Common Pitfalls
- Spray and pray: 200 applications without targeting is less effective than 30 targeted ones
- Not following up: 80% of candidates don't follow up. One polite follow-up increases response rate significantly.
- Underselling impact: "I helped with X" vs "I led X which resulted in Y"
- Waiting to network: Start networking before you urgently need a job

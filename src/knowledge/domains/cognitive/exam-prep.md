# Exam Preparation — Any Standardized Test (4–12 weeks)

## Phase 1: Diagnostic and Foundation (Weeks 1–2)
Never start studying without a baseline. A diagnostic exam tells you exactly where to spend your time.

Day 1–2: Full diagnostic exam under real conditions
- Take a complete practice test, timed, with no breaks beyond the real test's allowances
- Score it section by section
- Identify your 3 weakest areas — these get 60% of study time

Day 3–7: Study the test format, not just the content
- Learn exactly what each question type is testing
- For every question type, understand the trap answers (distractors are deliberately seductive)
- Do 20 practice questions per question type — analyze every wrong answer

Milestone: Understand why you got each diagnostic question wrong. "I didn't know it" vs "I knew it but misread" vs "I fell for a trap" — each requires a different fix.

Week 2: Build foundational content knowledge
- Identify core concepts that appear in 3+ question types
- Learn these cold — flashcards, not re-reading notes
- No practice tests this week — only targeted skill building

## Phase 2: Deliberate Practice (Weeks 3–8)
Deliberate practice principle (Ericsson): Work at the edge of your ability, get immediate feedback, correct errors. Passive re-reading is not deliberate practice.

Daily template: 25 min timed practice (question sets, not full tests) + 20 min error analysis + 15 min targeted concept review.

Weekly structure:
- Day 1–5: Work on two weakest areas identified in diagnostic
- Day 6: One full section timed — score and analyze
- Day 7: Review all errors from the week, identify patterns

Error analysis protocol (mandatory after every practice set):
1. For every wrong answer: write out WHY the correct answer is right
2. For every right answer you were unsure of: note the concept for review
3. Track error types in a spreadsheet — patterns emerge after 50+ errors

Milestone: Improvement of 10+ percentile points from diagnostic in your two weakest sections.

Spaced repetition for content knowledge:
- Day 1: Learn concept
- Day 3: Active recall test (no notes)
- Day 7: Active recall test again
- Day 21: Final recall test
- Concept is mastered only if you recall it correctly on Day 21

## Phase 3: Simulation and Confidence (Weeks 9–12)
Testing under real conditions is its own skill. The final 4 weeks are about stamina, pacing, and trust.

Week 9–10: Full practice tests
- 2 full timed practice tests per week
- Simulate exact test conditions: same time of day, same snacks, no phone
- After each test: 2-hour error review session
- Target: within 5-10% of goal score consistently

Week 11: Refinement
- Address any remaining persistent error patterns
- Focus 80% of time on medium-difficulty questions — easy are already solid, hardest have low ROI
- Mental preparation: review visualization and test-day routine

Week 12: Taper and test week
- Days 1–3: Light review only, no new practice tests
- Day 4–5: 30 min easy practice to stay sharp — stop at 30 min
- Day 6: Rest — sleep is the most important study tool
- Day 7: Exam day

## Test Day Execution
- Read every question stem twice before looking at answers
- Eliminate obviously wrong answers first
- Flag and skip questions that need more than 90 seconds — return at the end
- Never leave answers blank (no penalty on most tests)

## Common Pitfalls
- Re-reading notes (passive, not active recall)
- Doing practice questions without analyzing errors
- Cramming new content in final week instead of solidifying known content
- Underestimating stamina — a 3-hour test is physically demanding; train for it

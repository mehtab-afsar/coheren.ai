# Writing — Building a Writing Practice (Fiction, Non-Fiction, or Both)

## Phase 1: Volume Over Quality (Weeks 1–4)
The single biggest mistake new writers make is editing while they draft. These are separate cognitive modes and must be kept separate. The inner critic must be silenced during drafting.

Daily writing habit formation:
- Write for 20 minutes every day without stopping, deleting, or reading back
- Rule: pen/keyboard never stops moving — if stuck, write "I don't know what comes next" until ideas arrive
- Quantity target: 300-500 words per session (quality is irrelevant in this phase)
- Same time, same place, same ritual (coffee, music, nothing) — environment anchoring

Genre selection:
- Pick one: fiction (short stories), non-fiction (essays, personal writing), or articles/blog posts
- Do not switch genres in Phase 1 — you're building the habit, not finding your style yet
- Style emerges through volume — most writers find their voice after 50,000+ words

Week 1–2: Warm-up exercises
- Describe a room you're sitting in as if a stranger has never seen it — 200 words
- Write a scene from memory: something that happened to you — make it feel alive
- Rewrite the opening paragraph of a favorite book in your own words
- Milestone: 14 consecutive days of 20-minute writing sessions (skip = reset streak)

Week 3–4: Short completed pieces
- Write a complete short piece (300-800 words) — beginning, middle, end
- Draft only. No editing. Finish it regardless of quality.
- 4 complete pieces by end of week 4
- Milestone: 4 complete rough drafts exist. The point is completion, not quality.

## Phase 2: Craft Acquisition (Weeks 5–12)
After volume builds the habit, study craft to improve what you're already doing. Study and practice happen simultaneously.

Craft study schedule:
- Read one craft book per 4-week block (Bird by Bird, On Writing, Story, The Sense of Style)
- Apply one technique from the book in the same week you read it
- Annotate what makes good writing good: read 3 published pieces per week and mark every sentence that works and why

Technique focus areas by week:
- Weeks 5–6: Scene construction — where is the character, what do they want, what's in the way, what changes?
- Weeks 7–8: Voice — sentence rhythm, word choice, POV consistency
- Weeks 9–10: Dialogue — subtext (what they mean vs what they say), action beats, attribution
- Weeks 11–12: Structure — where to start a story, how to end it, pacing through scene length

Revision introduction (only in Phase 2):
- In week 6: revise your best rough draft from Phase 1
- Process: first read for structure, then read for scene, then read for sentence, then read for word
- Never revise and draft in the same session — pick one

Milestone: One revised piece you'd share with another writer. Not perfect — shareable.

## Phase 3: Feedback and Publishing (Weeks 13–24)
Writing without readers is practice. Writing for readers is development. Feedback, however uncomfortable, is what moves writers forward.

Feedback sources:
- Writing group: 3-5 writers exchanging work every 2 weeks (online or local)
- Beta readers: people who read your genre, not just supportive friends
- Critique partners: give and receive specific, craft-focused feedback
- Never ask "did you like it?" Ask "where did you lose interest?" "What confused you?" "What did you want more of?"

Feedback receipt protocol:
- Say thank you, take notes, ask clarifying questions — do not defend or explain
- Sit on feedback for 48 hours before deciding what to act on
- Not all feedback is right — pattern recognition: if 3 of 4 readers say the same thing, it's real

Publishing options (from lowest to highest bar):
- Personal blog or newsletter (immediate, no gatekeepers, builds audience)
- Literary magazines and journals (fiction and essays — start with small publications)
- Substack (newsletter platform with built-in discovery)
- Medium Partner Program (paid for reads)
- Traditional publishing (years of querying for books — requires completed manuscript)

Writing output targets:
- Month 3: 1 complete piece per week (rough draft)
- Month 4-6: 1 revised piece per month submitted somewhere (or published on own platform)
- Month 7-12: regular publishing cadence established (weekly newsletter, monthly essay, etc.)

Milestone: One piece published somewhere accessible to strangers. Even your own Substack counts.

## Common Pitfalls
- Editing while drafting: destroys momentum and trains self-censorship
- Waiting for inspiration: professional writers write on schedule, not on inspiration
- Perfectionism before sharing: no piece is ever finished, only abandoned (Paul Valéry). Share before perfect.
- Isolation: writing benefits enormously from community — other writers are your best resource

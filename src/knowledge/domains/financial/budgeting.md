# Budgeting and Personal Finance — Building Financial Control

## Phase 1: Financial Visibility (Weeks 1–3)
You cannot manage what you cannot see. Most people have no accurate picture of their spending — they guess, and the guess is always lower than reality.

Net worth calculation (Day 1):
- Assets: checking, savings, investments, property value, car value
- Liabilities: mortgage balance, auto loan, student loans, credit card balances, personal loans
- Net worth = Assets - Liabilities
- Track this number monthly. The direction matters, not the number.

Income and spending audit:
- Download 3 months of bank and credit card statements
- Categorize every transaction (most banks auto-categorize — review accuracy)
- Categories: Housing, Transport, Food (grocery separate from dining out), Entertainment, Subscriptions, Health, Clothing, Miscellaneous
- No judgment in this phase — only visibility

Surprise expenses identification:
- Annual expenses that catch people off-guard: car registration, insurance renewals, holiday gifts, vacations, medical deductibles
- Divide each by 12 and treat as monthly budget line items
- These are NOT emergencies — they're predictable irregular expenses

Milestone: Know exactly where last month's money went, categorized, to within 5%. Find one subscription you forgot you had.

## Phase 2: Budget Framework and Spending Plan (Weeks 4–8)
A budget is a spending plan, not a punishment. The goal is alignment between spending and values — spending more on what matters, less on what doesn't.

The 50/30/20 framework (starting point, not a rule):
- 50% Needs: housing, utilities, groceries, transport, minimum debt payments
- 30% Wants: dining out, entertainment, subscriptions, hobbies, shopping
- 20% Savings and extra debt payments: emergency fund, investments, retirement

Zero-based budgeting (more precise alternative):
- Every dollar of income is assigned a job before the month begins
- Income - (all categories including savings) = $0
- Excess does not go to "misc" — it gets assigned

Fixed vs variable expenses:
- Fixed (same every month): rent/mortgage, loan payments, insurance, subscriptions → automate
- Variable (changes monthly): groceries, gas, dining, entertainment → these need active management
- Most people have a variable expense problem, not a fixed expense problem

50/30/20 adjustment triggers:
- If housing > 30% of income: emergency level, prioritize income increase or location change
- If debt payments > 15% of income: avalanche payoff strategy immediately
- If savings < 10%: not sustainable long-term, reduce wants category

Milestone: First full month operating on a written budget. Track actual vs. planned for each category.

## Phase 3: Debt Elimination and Wealth Building (Weeks 9–24)
Debt payoff and wealth building happen in parallel once budget is under control and emergency fund exists.

Debt avalanche method (mathematically optimal):
- List all debts: balance, interest rate, minimum payment
- Pay minimums on all debts
- Direct every extra dollar to the highest-interest debt
- When that debt is paid: redirect its minimum payment plus extra to next highest
- The freed-up minimum payments accelerate the avalanche dramatically

Example avalanche ($3000 extra per month available for debt):
- Credit card A: $5,000 at 24% APR — payoff priority 1 → paid in ~2 months
- Credit card B: $8,000 at 19% APR — now redirect card A's minimum + extra → paid in ~3 months
- Car loan: $12,000 at 7% APR — redirect all above → paid in ~3 months
- Total payoff: ~8 months vs 3+ years making minimums only

Savings rate progression:
- Month 1-3: 5% savings rate (often the start for those in debt)
- Month 4-6: 10% savings rate (after smallest debts paid off)
- Month 7-12: 15-20% savings rate (avalanche working, cash flow improving)
- Year 2+: 20-30%+ savings rate (debt-free, compound growth accelerating)

Automating wealth building:
- Pay yourself first: savings comes out of paycheck before discretionary spending
- 401(k) contribution: direct from paycheck, never touches checking account
- IRA contribution: auto-transfer on payday
- Emergency fund: separate HYSA at different bank (prevents raiding it)

Milestone: Emergency fund fully funded (3 months expenses). One debt fully paid off. Savings rate above 15%.

## Common Pitfalls
- Budgeting without tracking: Creating a budget without reviewing actuals weekly is wishful thinking
- Undercounting irregular expenses: Car repairs, medical bills, and home maintenance always happen — plan for them
- Lifestyle inflation: Every income increase should go 50% to savings before upgrading lifestyle
- Saving before emergency fund: Investing while having no emergency fund means one crisis sells your investments at the worst time

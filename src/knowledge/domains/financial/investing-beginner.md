# Investing — Beginner to Competent Investor

## Phase 1: Financial Foundations (Weeks 1–4)
No investment strategy works on top of a broken financial foundation. Sequence matters: emergency fund → debt elimination → investment basics.

Emergency fund first:
- 3-6 months of essential expenses in a high-yield savings account
- No investments until this exists — a market dip can't force you to sell if you have cash reserves
- HYSA target yield: Fed funds rate - 0.5% (check current rates, not a fixed number)

Debt priority:
- High-interest debt (>7% APR): pay off before investing — guaranteed 7%+ return
- Student loans / mortgage (<5% APR): can invest alongside repayment
- Debt avalanche: pay minimums on all, then extra payments to highest-APR debt first

Core investment concepts (learn before touching money):
- Compound interest: $1000 at 7% for 30 years = $7,612. Time is the only variable you can't buy back.
- Inflation: your cash loses purchasing power at ~3% per year — keeping everything in savings has a guaranteed cost
- Risk/return tradeoff: higher expected returns require tolerating larger price swings
- Diversification: not putting eggs in one basket — but also not spreading so thin nothing matters

Paper trading before real money:
- Open a paper trading account (TD Ameritrade thinkorswim, Investopedia Stock Simulator)
- Execute all planned trades in simulation for 4 weeks before committing real money
- Goal: learn the mechanics, not generate returns

Milestone: Emergency fund funded. Paper trading account opened. Core concepts can be explained to a friend.

## Phase 2: Index Fund Foundation (Weeks 5–10)
For 90%+ of investors, index fund investing in tax-advantaged accounts outperforms stock picking. This is not a controversial position in finance — it is the conclusion of 50+ years of academic research.

Account types (USA context — tax optimization matters enormously):
- 401(k): Pre-tax contributions, employer match is free money (always contribute enough to get full match)
- Roth IRA: Post-tax contributions, tax-free growth and withdrawals — best for most earners under $160k
- Taxable brokerage: After-maxing-out tax-advantaged accounts

Index fund selection:
- Total US Market: VTI (Vanguard) or FSKAX (Fidelity)
- Total International: VXUS (Vanguard) or FZILX (Fidelity)
- Total Bond Market: BND (Vanguard) or FXNAX (Fidelity)
- Three-fund portfolio: US stocks + International stocks + Bonds covers 99% of the market

Asset allocation by risk tolerance and timeline:
- 20-30 years to retirement: 90% stocks / 10% bonds
- 10-20 years: 80% stocks / 20% bonds
- Under 10 years: 60% stocks / 40% bonds
- Rule of thumb starting point: "110 minus your age" as stock percentage

Dollar-cost averaging:
- Invest the same amount on the same day each month — regardless of market conditions
- Removes the paralysis of "should I invest now or wait?"
- Over time: buy more shares when prices are low, fewer when high — averages out favorably
- Automate: set up automatic investment so emotion never intervenes

Milestone: First real-money investment in a tax-advantaged account (even $100). Automation set up for monthly contributions.

## Phase 3: Tax Optimization and Portfolio Management (Weeks 11–20)
Taxes are the largest investment expense most people can control. Tax optimization compounds silently.

Tax-loss harvesting:
- Sell investments at a loss to offset capital gains elsewhere
- Immediately reinvest in similar (not identical) funds to maintain market exposure
- Saves up to 37% in taxes on capital gains depending on bracket

Rebalancing:
- Once or twice per year: check if allocation has drifted from target
- If stocks outperformed: sell some stocks, buy bonds (or direct new contributions to underweight asset)
- Emotional discipline: rebalancing forces you to "sell high, buy low" automatically

Tax-advantaged maximums (USA 2024):
- 401(k): $23,000 per year ($30,500 if 50+)
- IRA: $7,000 per year ($8,000 if 50+)
- HSA: $4,150 individual, $8,300 family (triple tax-advantaged: deductible, grows tax-free, withdrawals tax-free for medical)

What NOT to do:
- Panic selling in market downturns: markets have historically recovered from every crash. Time in market > timing the market.
- Checking portfolio daily: daily price fluctuations are noise. Monthly check is sufficient.
- Chasing performance: last year's best-performing fund is frequently next year's worst
- Individual stock picking without edge: 85% of actively managed funds underperform their index over 10 years

Milestone: Tax-advantaged accounts maximized (or on track to maximize). Annual rebalancing scheduled in calendar.

## Common Pitfalls
- Starting too complex: index funds first, everything else after you understand the basics
- Waiting for the "right time": time in market beats timing the market, always
- Ignoring fees: 1% annual fee vs 0.03% fee on $100k over 30 years = $300k difference
- Emotional decisions: a written investment policy statement (what you own, why, and when you'd change) prevents panic selling

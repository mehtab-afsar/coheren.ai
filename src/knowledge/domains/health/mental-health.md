# Mental Health Maintenance — Evidence-Based Daily Practices

## Phase 1: Baseline and Fundamental Practices (Weeks 1–4)
Mental health is not the absence of symptoms — it is a set of daily practices that build resilience, emotional regulation, and cognitive flexibility. These practices have the strongest evidence base across clinical literature.

The evidence hierarchy for mental health interventions:
1. Exercise (strongest evidence — equivalent to antidepressants for mild-moderate depression)
2. Sleep (bidirectional: poor sleep causes mental health issues; mental health issues cause poor sleep)
3. Social connection (loneliness is as harmful to health as smoking 15 cigarettes per day — Holt-Lunstad)
4. Sunlight exposure (20-30 min daily reduces depression risk by 35%)
5. Journaling and expressive writing (Pennebaker research: 20 min of expressive writing 3x = therapeutic effect)

Week 1–2: Physical foundation
- Exercise: 20-30 min moderate intensity, 3-5 days per week
- This is not optional — BDNF (brain-derived neurotrophic factor) from exercise is the closest thing to a natural antidepressant
- If motivation is low: commit to 5 min walking. Walking 5 min/day is categorically better than 0 min.

Week 3–4: Social and environmental practices
- One meaningful social interaction per day (in-person is stronger than digital, but digital counts)
- Sunlight within 30 min of waking, minimum 10 min outside daily
- Begin 5-minute daily reflection (what happened today? how did it feel? what would I do differently?)

Milestone: Exercise 3×/week maintained for 4 consecutive weeks. Daily reflection practiced 5 of 7 days.

## Phase 2: Emotional Regulation Skills (Weeks 5–10)
Emotional regulation is a learnable skill, not a fixed trait. The goal is not to eliminate negative emotions but to increase response flexibility — being able to choose how to respond rather than reacting automatically.

Mindfulness basics (secular, evidence-based):
- Attention training: notice where your attention is, redirect it intentionally
- Start: 5 min daily of focused breathing (Headspace, Waking Up, or self-guided)
- After 2 weeks: extend to 10 min
- Evidence: 8 weeks of 10 min/day mindfulness practice measurably changes amygdala reactivity

Cognitive reappraisal (from CBT):
- Identify automatic thoughts: when upset, write the thought down immediately ("I'm terrible at this")
- Challenge: Is this thought a fact or an interpretation? What evidence exists for and against?
- Reframe: "I'm struggling with this specific thing right now" vs "I'm terrible at this"
- Practice: write 1 thought + challenge per day for 21 days

The feelings wheel:
- Most people have a limited emotional vocabulary (happy, sad, angry, fine)
- Identifying emotions with precision ("I'm frustrated because I feel invisible in this meeting") reduces their intensity and creates response options
- Use a feelings wheel (downloadable free) to expand vocabulary

Journaling protocols that work:
- Gratitude: 3 specific things (not "family" — "my daughter's laugh when I tickle her today")
- Expressive writing: 20 min of uncensored writing about a stressor (Pennebaker protocol, 3x/week)
- Values journaling: "Did I act in alignment with my values today? Where did I fall short?"

Milestone: Mindfulness practice at 10 min/day for 21 consecutive days. One automatic thought challenged and reframed per day for 14 days.

## Phase 3: Resilience Building and Sustainable Maintenance (Weeks 11–24)
Resilience is not never struggling — it is the ability to recover from struggle faster. These practices build the capacity for recovery.

Stress inoculation:
- Controlled voluntary exposure to mild stress builds tolerance: cold showers, hard exercise, fasting for 16 hours
- Deliberate discomfort teaches the nervous system that distress is survivable and temporary
- Breath work for nervous system regulation: box breathing (4-4-4-4) activates parasympathetic state within 90 seconds

Professional support:
- Therapy is not only for crisis — it is maintenance care, like the gym for the mind
- Types: CBT (best evidence for anxiety, depression), DBT (emotional regulation), ACT (values-based behavior)
- If therapy is financially inaccessible: Workbook-based CBT (Feeling Good by David Burns) has strong evidence for self-directed use

Building a support network:
- Identify 3-5 people you can contact when struggling — these must be explicitly maintained relationships
- One relationship that requires vulnerability per week (share something real, not surface-level)
- Community engagement: belonging to something larger than yourself consistently predicts wellbeing

Review and adjust (quarterly):
- Assess: sleep, exercise, social connection, stress levels — these are the metrics
- What's working? What's missing? What's becoming automatic vs. requiring effort?

Milestone: One social relationship explicitly invested in per week. One professional support contact made (therapist, coach, or support group).

## Common Pitfalls
- Waiting until crisis: mental health practices are most effective as prevention, not rescue
- Avoiding discomfort: chronic avoidance of difficult emotions increases their power
- Social media as connection substitute: passive consumption of social media is not social connection — it often increases loneliness
- Inconsistency: daily practices work through accumulation. Missing 3 of 7 days halves the effect.

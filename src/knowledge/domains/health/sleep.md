# Sleep Optimization — Building Quality Sleep Consistently

## Phase 1: Sleep Audit and Circadian Rhythm (Weeks 1–2)
Sleep quality is determined more by circadian rhythm consistency than by any supplement or technique. The circadian clock is set primarily by light exposure. Every other sleep hack is secondary.

Sleep audit (Week 1):
- Track: sleep time, wake time, sleep quality (1-10), morning alertness (1-10), afternoon energy
- Tools: free sleep journal (notes app), Oura Ring, Apple Watch, or Garmin (for data)
- Key metrics: sleep latency (time to fall asleep), wake-after-sleep-onset (WASO), consistency of wake time

Circadian science:
- Your circadian rhythm has a phase set by light and reset daily
- Morning light (10-30 min within 1 hour of waking) sets the cortisol awakening response — this is the anchor for the entire day's rhythm
- Evening light (screens, bright lights after sunset) delays melatonin production by 90-150 min
- Consistent wake time matters more than consistent bedtime — the wake time anchors the rhythm

Week 1: Morning light protocol
- Wake at the same time daily (even weekends)
- Within 30 minutes: go outside or stand by a window for 10-15 minutes
- Do not wear sunglasses (eye exposure required — not skin)
- Overcast days: 15-20 min. Direct sun: 5-10 min.
- Milestone: 7 consecutive days of same wake time + morning light

Week 2: Evening light reduction
- Dim household lights after 8 PM (or 2 hours before bedtime)
- Blue light filter on all screens (Night Shift on iOS/Mac, f.lux on Windows)
- Ideally: stop screen use 60 min before bed — audiobooks, reading physical books, or conversation instead
- Milestone: Track sleep quality before and after implementation. Any improvement confirms the intervention is working.

## Phase 2: Sleep Architecture and Environment (Weeks 3–6)
Sleep occurs in cycles of ~90 minutes: light sleep → deep sleep (slow-wave) → REM. Deep sleep (first half of night) is physically restorative. REM (second half) is cognitively restorative. Both require sufficient total sleep time (7-9 hours) and undisrupted architecture.

Temperature:
- Core body temperature must drop 1-3°F to initiate and maintain sleep
- Bedroom temperature: 65-68°F (18-20°C) is optimal for most people
- Warm bath or shower 60-90 min before bed: paradoxically helps — blood rushes to skin surface, causing core temperature to drop afterward

Noise:
- White noise machine or fan: masks variable noise (neighbors, traffic) better than silence for light sleepers
- Earplugs: effective but can cause anxiety in parents or people who need to hear alarms
- Target: consistent ambient noise level, not silence

Light:
- Complete darkness is strongly associated with better sleep quality
- Blackout curtains or sleep mask — even small amounts of light reduce melatonin
- No light-emitting devices facing the bed (router lights, TV standby, charger lights — cover with tape)

Wind-down routine (20-30 minutes before sleep):
- Same 3-5 activities in the same order — signals the brain that sleep is coming
- Examples: tea (herbal, not caffeinated), reading, journaling, light stretching
- Avoid: news, work email, emotionally activating conversations, vigorous exercise
- Milestone: 14-day streak of wind-down routine. Rate sleep quality before vs. after.

## Phase 3: Advanced Optimization (Weeks 7–12)
Once the foundations are working, targeted interventions can further improve deep sleep and REM.

Caffeine timing:
- Adenosine (sleep pressure) accumulates throughout the day and is cleared during sleep
- Caffeine blocks adenosine receptors — it doesn't reduce sleepiness, it masks it
- Caffeine half-life: 5-7 hours. Quarter-life: 10-14 hours.
- Last caffeine by 1 PM for most people (adjust: if bedtime is 11 PM, stop caffeine by 1-2 PM)

Alcohol:
- Alcohol is a sedative, not a sleep aid — it sedates, which is not the same as sleep
- Even 2 drinks reduces slow-wave sleep by 20% and REM by 30%
- If drinking: minimum 4 hours between last drink and sleep

Exercise timing:
- Exercise improves sleep quality — even 20 min of moderate exercise per day
- Vigorous exercise within 2 hours of bed elevates cortisol — avoid or move to earlier
- Morning exercise: strongest positive effect on evening sleep quality

Supplements (evidence-ranked):
- Magnesium glycinate (300-400mg): small but real effect on sleep quality. Well-tolerated. Evidence: moderate.
- Melatonin (0.3-1mg, NOT the typical 5-10mg): helps with circadian phase shifting (jet lag, shift work). Does not improve sleep quality at high doses — lower doses are more effective.
- Chamomile, valerian: mild anxiolytic effects. Evidence: weak but real.
- Avoid: alcohol, antihistamines (Benadryl), cannabis — all suppress REM

Milestone: Average sleep quality ≥7/10 for 2 consecutive weeks. Consistent wake time maintained 6 of 7 days.

## Common Pitfalls
- Weekend sleep schedule variability: sleeping 2+ hours later on weekends is called "social jet lag" — undoes the entire week's circadian work
- Over-reliance on supplements: sleep hygiene matters 10x more than any supplement
- Tracking-induced anxiety: obsessing over Oura/Garmin scores worsens sleep. Use data for trends, not judgment.
- Giving up after 3 days: circadian rhythm takes 1-2 weeks to shift. Changes won't feel immediate.

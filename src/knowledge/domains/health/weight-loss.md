# Weight Loss — Evidence-Based Fat Loss

## Phase 1: Measurement and Foundation (Weeks 1–3)
Weight loss is driven by one variable: sustained caloric deficit over time. But sustainable deficit requires understanding personal baseline and building habits that don't require constant willpower.

Baseline measurement:
- TDEE (Total Daily Energy Expenditure): use a calculator (Mifflin-St Jeor formula + activity multiplier)
- Track everything you eat for 7 days WITHOUT changing anything — just observe
- Most people underestimate daily intake by 20-40% before tracking
- Target deficit: 300-500 calories per day — produces 0.3-0.5 kg (0.6-1 lb) loss per week

Weight tracking correctly:
- Weigh daily, first thing in morning, after bathroom, before eating
- Track weekly average, not daily number (daily fluctuations of 1-2 kg from water, food, and hormones are normal)
- Progress photos every 4 weeks — the scale doesn't capture body composition changes

Protein priority:
- Protein is the most important macro during fat loss
- Target: 1.6-2.2g per kg of bodyweight per day
- Protein increases satiety, preserves muscle mass during deficit, and has highest thermic effect (25% of protein calories burned in digestion)
- Practical: 30-40g protein at every main meal

Week 1: Tracking and baseline only
- Log every food and drink in MyFitnessPal or Cronometer
- No food restrictions yet — just visibility
- Milestone: 7 consecutive days of logging. Know your average daily caloric intake.

Week 2–3: Introduce deficit
- Reduce intake by 300-400 calories from baseline (not all at once — reduce by 100 cal every 3 days)
- Prioritize protein: if protein is low, increase it before reducing anything else
- Milestone: Deficit maintained for 7 days. Weekly weight average compared to week 1.

## Phase 2: Sustainable Habits and Progress (Weeks 4–12)
A deficit you can maintain for 90 days produces more results than a severe deficit maintained for 10 days. Sustainability > intensity.

Food strategies that reduce hunger without counting (after baseline understanding):
- Volume eating: fill half the plate with non-starchy vegetables (low calorie, high fiber, high volume)
- Protein first: eat protein before carbohydrates in every meal (reduces total intake naturally)
- Eliminate liquid calories: juice, soda, alcohol, and fancy coffees can account for 500+ cal/day
- Whole foods over processed: whole foods are more filling per calorie than processed equivalents

Movement integration:
- Non-Exercise Activity Thermogenesis (NEAT): all movement outside formal exercise
- Target: 8,000-10,000 steps per day — this alone creates a meaningful caloric deficit
- Resistance training during fat loss: preserves muscle (muscle burns calories at rest — protect it)
- Cardio: useful but not required. 150 min/week moderate intensity is the evidence-based target.

Hunger management:
- Hunger is not an emergency. A 300-500 cal deficit will produce mild hunger at certain times — this is normal.
- Hunger peaks and passes: wait 20 minutes before eating in response to hunger
- High-fiber foods slow digestion and extend satiety: legumes, oats, vegetables, fruit
- Sleep: 7-8 hours reduces ghrelin (hunger hormone) and increases leptin (satiety hormone)

Plateau management:
- Weight loss plateaus are universal — they do not mean the diet stopped working
- First: verify tracking accuracy (people underestimate intake more during plateaus)
- Second: recalculate TDEE (weight loss reduces TDEE — fewer kg = fewer calories needed)
- Third: diet break for 2 weeks at maintenance (reduces metabolic adaptation)

Milestone: 4-week weight average is lower than the starting 4-week average. Habits (protein, logging, steps) are automatic.

## Phase 3: Final Push and Maintenance Transition (Weeks 13–24)
The last 20% of goal weight is the hardest: hunger hormones increase, metabolism adapts, and motivation wanes. The plan must anticipate this.

Advanced strategies for the final phase:
- Caloric cycling: eat at deficit 5 days, maintenance 2 days (psychological relief + metabolic adaptation prevention)
- Protein increase: as deficit continues, increase protein to 2.2-2.5g/kg to protect muscle
- Resistance training increase: heavier weights during fat loss phase preserves metabolic rate

Maintenance transition (critical — most people regain weight here):
- Do not stop all tracking immediately after reaching goal
- Increase calories by 100-150 per week until weight is stable (4-6 weeks)
- New maintenance calories are often 100-200 below original TDEE estimate (body adapts)
- Keep the habit structures: protein targets, vegetable intake, steps — these become your maintenance plan

Body recomposition (simultaneous fat loss and muscle gain):
- Possible for beginners and people returning from a break
- Requires: slight caloric deficit + heavy resistance training + high protein
- Progress is slower than either pure cutting or bulking but body composition improves

Milestone: Goal weight reached and maintained for 4 weeks. Maintenance calorie intake identified and stable.

## Common Pitfalls
- Too aggressive a deficit: below 1200 cal (women) or 1500 cal (men) causes muscle loss, fatigue, and rebound
- Ignoring protein: losing weight without protein = losing muscle, not fat
- All-or-nothing thinking: one bad meal is not failure. The next meal is the reset button.
- Overestimating exercise: 30 min of running burns ~250-300 calories — one cookie replaces it

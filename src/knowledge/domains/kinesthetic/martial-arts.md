# Martial Arts / Combat Sports — Beginner Fundamentals

## Phase 1: Basic Mechanics and Safety (Weeks 1–6)
Every strike, kick, and grappling technique has a biomechanical foundation. Learning technique before power is not optional — bad habits ingrained early are extremely difficult to unlearn after 100+ hours of practice.

Boxing stance fundamentals:
- Dominant hand back (orthodox: right-handed → left foot forward, southpaw: left-handed → right foot forward)
- Feet shoulder-width apart, weight 60% back foot, 40% front foot
- Knees slightly bent, weight on balls of feet (never flat-footed)
- Hands at cheekbone height, elbows covering ribs, chin tucked to chest
- Eyes on opponent's chest (periphery catches shoulder and hip telegraphing)

The Jab (most important punch to learn):
- Step-punch coordination: front foot steps as hand extends
- Lead shoulder rotates forward as jab extends — "close the gap between ear and shoulder"
- Full extension creates power — not just shoulder push, use the rotation
- Retract along the same path, faster than you threw it
- Return to guard immediately — every punch starts and ends at guard

Week 1–2: Stance and footwork only
- 10 min stance drill: hold guard in mirror, check every point, reset 50 times
- Shadow footwork: advance, retreat, lateral movement — stay on balls of feet
- Never cross your feet — step-step, not stride
- Milestone: Move around for 3 minutes maintaining correct stance without reminders

Week 3–4: Jab and cross
- Jab: 100 reps daily on mirror shadow boxing — no bag yet
- Cross (rear hand): full hip rotation, rear heel pivots as you throw
- 1-2 combination: jab sets up the cross. 200 reps per day.
- Milestone: 1-2 combination on bag with audible pop on contact (not a push)

Week 5–6: Defense basics
- Slip: move head offline as punch comes — slip outside or inside
- Bob and weave: bend knees (not waist), move head under a hook
- Parry: redirect incoming jab with lead hand — don't swipe, redirect
- Milestone: Partner throws 10 slow jabs, slip all 10 (both sides)

## Phase 2: Combinations and Timing (Weeks 7–14)
Punches land because of timing and setup, not power. The jab creates the opening the cross closes.

Combination progressions:
- 1-2-3 (jab-cross-left hook): cross sets up the angle for the hook
- 1-2-3-2 (add rear cross after hook): hooks create opening for cross
- Body-head: punch to body to bring guard down, punch to head to finish
- Double jab: rhythm change to disrupt opponent's timing

Bag work sessions (structured):
- 3 minutes: jab only — focus on snap, retraction, footwork
- 3 minutes: 1-2 combinations — rhythm and power
- 3 minutes: freestyle — vary combinations, move between combinations
- 3 minutes: defensive focus — jab, slip, counter 1-2
- Rest 1 minute between rounds

Partner work (with coach or partner):
- Mitt work: Coach calls combinations — trains reaction and accuracy
- Slow sparring: 30-40% speed, focus on technique not winning
- No full-contact sparring before 3-4 months of consistent training

Milestone: Complete 6 rounds of bag work without significant form breakdown.

## Phase 3: Sparring Introduction and Specialization (Weeks 15–24)
Sparring is where technique meets pressure. The goal is not to win — it is to practice what you've drilled.

Sparring rules for beginners:
- Never go above 50% power for first 3 months of sparring
- Start with partners who will work with you, not against you
- Signal to stop: verbal "stop" or double-tap to partner or mat
- Guard up between every exchange — reset to guard after every combination

Distance management:
- Three ranges: long (jab range), medium (cross and hook range), close (uppercut range)
- Learn to change range deliberately — this is footwork applied to fighting

Conditioning requirements:
- 3 × 3-minute rounds of bag work feels like 15 min of running — cardiovascular base matters
- Jump rope 10 min per day: footwork, coordination, and conditioning simultaneously

Milestone: 3 rounds of light technical sparring (30-40% power) where you execute at least 3 planned combinations.

## Common Pitfalls
- Brawling in sparring: Ego causes injury. Technical sparring is the goal — not fighting.
- Skipping footwork: Power without footwork is just pushing. Footwork generates angles and power.
- Dropping guard after punching: Every punch must return to guard before the next one starts.
- Training without drilling: Random bag hitting builds bad habits. Structured drilling builds muscle memory.

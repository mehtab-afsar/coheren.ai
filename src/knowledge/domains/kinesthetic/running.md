# Running — Couch to 5K and Beyond

## Phase 1: Foundation — Run/Walk to Continuous Running (Weeks 1–4)
The biggest mistake beginners make is running too fast, too far, too soon. 80% of running injuries are overuse injuries caused by increasing volume or intensity too quickly. The 10% rule: never increase weekly mileage by more than 10% per week.

Conversational pace test: You should be able to hold a short sentence while running. If you can't, slow down — you're not building aerobic base, you're accumulating debt.

Week 1–2: Run/walk intervals
- Day 1: 1 min run / 2 min walk × 8 = 24 min total
- Day 3: 1 min run / 90s walk × 10 = 25 min total
- Day 5: 2 min run / 2 min walk × 6 = 24 min total
- Milestone: Complete all intervals without stopping ahead of schedule

Week 3–4: Extend run intervals
- Day 1: 3 min run / 90s walk × 6 = 27 min total
- Day 3: 4 min run / 1 min walk × 5 = 25 min total
- Day 5: 5 min run / 1 min walk × 4 = 24 min total
- Milestone: Run 5 minutes continuously at conversational pace without stopping

Running form checkpoints:
- Foot strike: Land under your hips, not heel-first in front of your body
- Cadence: Aim for 170-180 steps per minute (use a metronome app)
- Arms: Elbows at 90 degrees, hands loose, arms driving forward-back not side-to-side
- Posture: Slight forward lean from ankles (not waist), eyes 10-15 meters ahead

## Phase 2: Building Continuous Running (Weeks 5–8)
Move from intervals to continuous runs. Heart rate zone 2 (60-70% max HR) is the target — this is the aerobic fat-burning zone that builds endurance.

Max HR estimate: 220 - age. Zone 2 = 60-70% of that number.

Week 5–6: First continuous runs
- 3 runs per week: 20 min, 25 min, 30 min (all at conversational pace)
- If you need to walk, walk — but restart running after 90 seconds
- Add one strength session: squats, lunges, calf raises (injury prevention)
- Milestone: Complete a 30-minute continuous run

Week 7–8: Build to 5K
- Long run: 35 min → 40 min → 45 min (add 5 min per week)
- Easy runs: 2 × 25 min at very easy pace (could hold a full conversation)
- Rest days: minimum 1 full rest day between runs
- Milestone: Run 5K (3.1 miles) continuously

## Phase 3: 5K to 10K (Weeks 9–16)
Once you can run 5K, the base is established. Now: volume before speed.

Weekly structure:
- Long run: Build from 5K to 10K over 8 weeks (add 1K per week)
- Easy runs: 2 × 30-35 min at conversational pace
- Optional: 1 tempo run per week (slightly faster than conversational, you can speak a word but not a sentence)

Speed should not be introduced until you can run 60 minutes continuously. Speed work on an insufficient aerobic base causes injury.

Milestone: Complete a 10K race or 10K training run.

## Injury Prevention Protocol
The most common beginner injuries: shin splints, runner's knee, IT band syndrome, plantar fasciitis. All are overuse injuries.

Prevention rules:
- Replace shoes every 400-500 miles (worn shoes cause most overuse injuries)
- Warm up: 5 min brisk walk before running, dynamic stretches (leg swings, hip circles)
- Cool down: 5 min walking, static stretches held 30 seconds each
- If it hurts: stop. Pain is not the same as discomfort. Discomfort = normal. Sharp or persistent pain = stop.
- Rest day rule: at least 2 full rest days per week for beginners

## Common Pitfalls
- Running too fast: Most beginner runs should feel embarrassingly slow
- Skipping rest days: Adaptation happens during rest, not during runs
- Comparing pace: Your 5K time is irrelevant. Consistency is the only metric that matters in Phase 1.
- Ignoring the warmup: Cold muscles running at full effort is how IT band syndrome starts

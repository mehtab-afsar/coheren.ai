# Strength Training — Beginner to Intermediate (3–6 months)

## Phase 1: Movement Foundation (Weeks 1–4)
Beginners make strength gains rapidly through neurological adaptation (better motor unit recruitment), not muscle growth. The first 4 weeks are about learning movement patterns with perfect form before adding load.

The 6 fundamental movements every program should include:
1. Squat (quad-dominant lower body)
2. Hip hinge (hamstring/glute-dominant — deadlift, Romanian deadlift)
3. Push (horizontal — bench press, push-up; vertical — overhead press)
4. Pull (horizontal — row; vertical — pull-up, lat pulldown)
5. Carry (farmer's walk, suitcase carry — core stability)
6. Core (plank, dead bug — anti-rotation)

Week 1–2: Bodyweight mastery
- Squat: 3 × 10 bodyweight, focusing on depth (hip crease below knee), knees tracking toes
- Push-up: 3 × max reps, chest touches floor, elbows at 45 degrees (not flared)
- Romanian deadlift: 3 × 10 with light dumbbells — hinge at hip, not round the back
- Plank: 3 × 30 seconds, body in straight line, posterior pelvic tilt
- Milestone: All movements feel controlled with no compensation patterns

Week 3–4: Introduction to barbells or machines
- Learn barbell back squat setup: bar on traps, brace core, sit back and down
- Learn barbell deadlift: conventional stance, bar over mid-foot, neutral spine throughout
- Learn bench press: arch, leg drive, bar path (slight diagonal — not straight up)
- 3 sets of 5 reps at very light weight — form over load, always
- Milestone: Complete 5 reps of each barbell lift with coachable form (record on video)

Form cues for squat: "Big chest, elbows down, knees out, sit back, drive through mid-foot"
Form cues for deadlift: "Bar close, big breath in, brace like you're about to get punched, push the floor away"
Form cues for bench: "Shoulder blades together and down, arch, bar path toward lower chest"

## Phase 2: Progressive Overload (Weeks 5–16)
Progressive overload is the engine of strength. You must consistently add weight, reps, or sets over time. Without a tracking system, progress stalls.

The most effective beginner program structure (linear periodization):
- 3 days per week, full body (not splits)
- Core lifts: Squat, Deadlift, Bench, Row, Overhead Press
- Rep range: 5×5 or 3×8 — pick one and stick to it for 8 weeks

Linear progression protocol:
- Every session: add 2.5kg (5 lbs) to upper body lifts, 5kg (10 lbs) to lower body lifts
- When you fail to complete all sets: deload 10%, rebuild
- Track every session: date, exercise, weight, sets, reps, notes

Sample 3-day full body program:
Day A: Squat 3×5 → Bench 3×5 → Barbell Row 3×5
Day B: Squat 3×5 → Overhead Press 3×5 → Deadlift 1×5
Alternate A-B-A one week, B-A-B the next.

Milestone at week 12: Squat bodyweight for reps, deadlift 1.25× bodyweight, bench 0.75× bodyweight.

Nutrition non-negotiables:
- Protein: 1.6–2.2g per kg of bodyweight per day (muscle synthesis research consensus)
- Caloric surplus for muscle growth: 200-300 cal above maintenance
- Sleep: 7-9 hours — growth hormone peaks during deep sleep

## Phase 3: Intermediate Programming (Weeks 17–24)
Linear progression stalls around week 12-16. Switch to weekly rather than session-by-session progression.

Texas Method (volume-intensity weekly wave):
- Volume day (Monday): 5×5 at 80-90% of max — accumulate fatigue
- Recovery day (Wednesday): 2×5 at 70% — active recovery, no new stress
- Intensity day (Friday): Work up to 1×5 personal record (new PR attempt)

Accessory work (added after core lifts):
- Lat pulldowns, face pulls, curls, tricep pushdowns — 3×10-12, not to failure
- These build muscle and address imbalances — do not skip

Milestone: First actual 1-rep max (1RM) test in all four primary lifts. Record and celebrate.

## Common Pitfalls
- Ego lifting: Using weight you can't control. Form breaks = injury waiting to happen.
- Not tracking: If you don't track, you don't progress. Every set, every rep.
- Skipping legs: Squat and deadlift release more growth hormone than any upper body lift. Leg day is non-negotiable.
- Inconsistency: 3 mediocre consistent months beats 3 perfect weeks. Show up even on bad days.
- Cardio timing: Never do intense cardio before strength training. Do it after, or on separate days.

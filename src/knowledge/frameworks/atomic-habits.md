# Atomic Habits — James Clear

## Four Laws of Behavior Change
Every habit follows four laws that can be used to build good habits or break bad ones.
1. MAKE IT OBVIOUS (Cue) — Use implementation intentions: "I will [BEHAVIOR] at [TIME] in [LOCATION]"
2. MAKE IT ATTRACTIVE (Craving) — Temptation bundling; join a culture where the desired behavior is the norm
3. MAKE IT EASY (Response) — Reduce friction; use the 2-minute rule to lower the barrier to starting
4. MAKE IT SATISFYING (Reward) — Immediate rewards; habit tracking; never miss twice
To break bad habits, invert all four: make it invisible, unattractive, difficult, unsatisfying.

## The Two-Minute Rule
When starting a new habit, scale it down to two minutes or less.
"Read before bed" → "Read one page." "Run 5km" → "Put on running shoes." "Study for exam" → "Open your notes."
This removes the motivation barrier. Master the art of showing up first, then optimize the process.
The goal is to standardize before you optimize. You can't improve a habit you don't do.

## Habit Stacking
Link new habits to existing ones using the formula: "After I [CURRENT HABIT], I will [NEW HABIT]."
Examples: After I pour my morning coffee, I will write one sentence. After I sit at my desk, I will write my #1 priority. After I brush my teeth, I will meditate for two minutes.
The existing habit serves as a reliable cue because it is already embedded in your schedule.

## Identity-Based Habits
The most effective behavior change comes from identity, not outcomes.
"I want to lose weight" → "I am a healthy person." "I want to read more" → "I am a reader."
Every action is a vote for the type of person you want to become. Focus on who you wish to become, not what you want to achieve. Two people refuse a cigarette — one says "No thanks, I'm trying to quit." The other says "No thanks, I'm not a smoker."

## The 1% Rule and Compounding
Getting 1% better each day compounds to 37x improvement over a year. Getting 1% worse every day shrinks you to nearly zero.
Small habits seem insignificant in the moment but compound into remarkable results. Progress is not linear — it is exponential, with a long "Plateau of Latent Potential" before results become visible.
The ice cube analogy: the cube does not melt at 25°, 26°, 27°C. But at 32°C it liquefies. The work was not wasted — it was just building up to the breakthrough.

## Never Miss Twice
Missing one day is an accident. Missing two days is the start of a new habit.
The first rule of habit recovery is to never miss twice. When you miss, the goal is to get back on track as quickly as possible. A bad workout is infinitely better than a skipped workout.

## Environment Design
Make the cue for good habits obvious and visible. Move the book to your pillow. Put the guitar in the middle of the room. Make the fruit bowl the first thing you see.
For bad habits: make the cue invisible. Delete the app. Remove the junk food from sight. Redesign your environment so that the good behavior requires less friction.

# CBT Cognitive Distortions — Beck & Burns

## What Cognitive Distortions Are
Cognitive distortions are systematic errors in thinking that maintain negative emotional states and maladaptive behavior patterns. They are not random mistakes — they are predictable, category-specific thought patterns that can be identified, named, and restructured using Cognitive Behavioral Therapy techniques.

In the context of habit change and goal pursuit, cognitive distortions are the primary mechanism through which the FearOfFailure, Perfectionism, and LowConfidence stones operate. Naming the distortion is the first step to interrupting its effect.

Source: Beck, A.T. (1979). Cognitive Therapy of Depression. Burns, D.D. (1980). Feeling Good: The New Mood Therapy.

## The Core Distortions Relevant to Goal Pursuit

### All-or-Nothing Thinking (Black-and-White Thinking)
Evaluating experience in absolute, binary categories. "I either do it perfectly or I've failed." "I missed one workout, so the whole week is ruined." No middle ground exists in the thinking.

Direct link to stones: The primary cognitive substrate of Perfectionism. Also underlies Inconsistency when users catastrophize a single skip into evidence that they "always" fail.

Restructuring: The gray scale. "I did 4 of 7 days — that is a 57% success rate on a new behavior, which is above baseline for week one." Partial completion is real progress. Perfect execution is statistically improbable during habit acquisition.

### Catastrophizing (Magnification)
Exaggerating the negative consequences of a setback. "If I submit this imperfect work, my reputation will be destroyed." "If I miss this deadline, everything falls apart."

Linked to: FearOfFailure (the imagined consequences of failure are grossly overestimated relative to actual risk).

Restructuring: The realistic probability chain. Walk through the actual sequence of events that would lead to the feared outcome. In most cases, the chain breaks down quickly because multiple improbable events would need to co-occur.

### Mind Reading
Assuming you know what others think about your performance or behavior without direct evidence. "They definitely noticed how bad my presentation was." "Everyone will judge me for starting over."

Linked to: FearOfFailure, Perfectionism. Operates as a social surveillance system that is almost always harsher than reality.

Restructuring: Evidence inventory. What actual evidence exists for the negative interpretation? What is the simpler, neutral explanation? People are far more focused on themselves than on your performance.

### Fortune Telling (Predictive Thinking)
Predicting negative outcomes as if they were facts. "I know I'll give up after a week." "I'm not going to be good at this." "It won't work for me."

Linked to: LowConfidence, FearOfFailure. Fortune telling pre-empts effort by generating pseudo-evidence against trying.

Restructuring: Treat predictions as hypotheses, not facts. "I predict I'll give up — what would be evidence that this prediction is wrong? Let me commit to three days and see what the data shows."

### Should/Must Statements (Tyranny of the Shoulds)
Rigid internal rules about how one must perform. "I should be able to do this already." "I must not ask for help — that's weakness." "I shouldn't need to take breaks."

Linked to: Perfectionism (the rules enforce an impossible standard), Overcommitment (should statements drive unsustainable effort levels).

Restructuring: Replace with preference language. "I would prefer to be further along" is empirically accurate and emotionally neutral. "I should be further along" creates shame without useful information.

### Labeling (Global Negative Judgment)
Attaching a global, stable character label to a specific behavioral event. Failing at one habit → "I'm a failure." Being inconsistent once → "I'm an undisciplined person." Struggling with a skill → "I'm just not talented."

Linked to: Inconsistency (each relapse is interpreted as evidence of a fixed character flaw), LowConfidence (accumulated labels build a negative self-theory).

Restructuring: Behavior-specific language. "I didn't follow through on that plan" vs. "I am someone who doesn't follow through." The first describes a behavior (changeable). The second describes a person (apparently fixed).

### Emotional Reasoning
Taking emotions as evidence of objective reality. "I feel like a failure, therefore I am failing." "I feel overwhelmed, therefore this is too much for me." "I feel afraid, therefore this is dangerous."

Linked to: FearOfFailure, LowConfidence. Emotional states color the interpretation of objective performance data.

Restructuring: Separate evidence from interpretation. "I feel overwhelmed AND the objective data shows I completed 4 of 5 tasks this week." Feelings are real; they are not always accurate reports on external reality.

### Overgeneralization
Drawing broad conclusions from single events. "It didn't work today" → "It never works." "One person didn't respond well" → "Nobody responds well to me."

Linked to: Inconsistency (one lapse becomes a pattern), LowConfidence (one failure becomes general incompetence).

Restructuring: Quantify and specify. "In how many instances did it not work? 1? 3? Out of how many attempts? What are the common conditions when it does work?"

### Mental Filter (Selective Abstraction)
Focusing exclusively on negative details while ignoring positive ones. One critical comment received after ten positive ones dominates memory. One bad week in a good month defines the month.

Linked to: Perfectionism (negative information is evidence that the standard wasn't met; positive information is not credited because it was expected), FearOfFailure.

Restructuring: Full data review. "What is the complete evidence set? What am I leaving out?"

### Disqualifying the Positive
Actively rejecting positive experiences by reframing them as invalid. "I only succeeded because the task was easy." "They only said that to be nice." "Anyone could have done that."

Linked to: LowConfidence (systematically prevents efficacy-building mastery experiences from registering), Perfectionism.

Restructuring: Apply the same evidentiary standard to positive and negative evidence. If success "doesn't count," the reasons why must be specific and falsifiable.

## Behavioral Interventions Alongside Cognitive Restructuring

### Behavioral Experiments
Instead of arguing about the distortion, design a real-world test. "You predict people will judge you for sharing imperfect work. Let's share one piece of work this week and record what actually happens." Behavioral data overrides cognitive prediction.

### Graded Task Assignment
For catastrophizing and all-or-nothing thinking: break the feared behavior into steps small enough that the catastrophized outcome cannot occur. One small proof-of-concept disconfirms the catastrophe.

### Activity Scheduling with Mastery/Pleasure Ratings
Track actual vs. predicted enjoyment and accomplishment for tasks. Users with emotional reasoning and fortune-telling consistently find that their predictions of how bad tasks will feel are worse than the actual experience.

## Application to Stone-Specific Coaching
- Perfectionism: Core distortions are All-or-Nothing, Should/Must, Mental Filter. Target these three with behavioral experiments in Foundation phase.
- FearOfFailure: Core distortions are Catastrophizing, Mind Reading, Fortune Telling. Graded exposure + behavioral prediction tracking are primary tools.
- LowConfidence: Core distortions are Labeling, Disqualifying the Positive, Emotional Reasoning. Mastery experience documentation is critical.
- Inconsistency: Core distortions are Overgeneralization and Labeling. Each relapse is interpreted as identity evidence; interrupt with behavior-specific language.

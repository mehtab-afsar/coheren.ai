# Four Laws of Behavior Change
**Source:** James Clear - Atomic Habits

## Core Principle
Every habit follows a 4-step pattern: Cue → Craving → Response → Reward.
To build good habits, make each step easier. To break bad habits, make each step harder.

## The Four Laws

### Law 1: Make It Obvious (Cue)

**To build a habit:**
- Use implementation intentions: "I will [BEHAVIOR] at [TIME] in [LOCATION]"
- Use habit stacking: "After [CURRENT HABIT], I will [NEW HABIT]"
- Design your environment (make cues visible)

**To break a habit:**
- Remove cues from your environment
- Reduce exposure to triggers

**Implementation Intentions Examples:**
- "I will meditate for 5 minutes at 7am in my bedroom"
- "I will study for 30 minutes at 6pm at my desk"
- "I will exercise for 20 minutes at 5pm in my living room"

**Habit Stacking Examples:**
- After I pour my morning coffee, I will write in my journal
- After I sit down for dinner, I will say one thing I'm grateful for
- After I take off my work shoes, I will change into workout clothes

### Law 2: Make It Attractive (Craving)

**To build a habit:**
- Temptation bundling: pair something you need to do with something you want to do
- Join a culture where your desired behavior is normal
- Create a motivation ritual before difficult habits

**To break a habit:**
- Reframe your mindset (highlight benefits of avoiding)
- Associate the habit with negative feelings

**Temptation Bundling:**
- Only listen to favorite podcasts while exercising
- Only watch TV while on the treadmill
- Only eat at favorite restaurant after finishing a project

**Social Environment:**
- Join a running club to become a runner
- Study with ambitious students to study more
- Work in a coworking space to be more productive

### Law 3: Make It Easy (Response)

**To build a habit:**
- Reduce friction (decrease steps between you and good habits)
- Prime your environment (prepare in advance)
- Use the Two-Minute Rule: downscale habits to 2 minutes
- Master the habit of showing up

**To break a habit:**
- Increase friction (add steps between you and bad habits)
- Use commitment devices

**Two-Minute Rule Examples:**
| Goal | 2-Minute Version |
|------|------------------|
| Read more | Read one page |
| Run a marathon | Put on running shoes |
| Study for class | Open your notes |
| Meditate | Sit in meditation position |
| Write a book | Write one sentence |

**Reducing Friction:**
- Lay out workout clothes the night before
- Keep healthy snacks at eye level
- Delete social media apps from phone
- Use website blockers during work hours

### Law 4: Make It Satisfying (Reward)

**To build a habit:**
- Give yourself an immediate reward
- Use habit tracking ("don't break the chain")
- Never miss twice

**To break a habit:**
- Make it immediately unsatisfying
- Get an accountability partner
- Create a habit contract

**Immediate Rewards:**
- After workout: enjoy a smoothie
- After study session: 10 minutes of phone time
- After completing task: check it off visibly

**Habit Tracking:**
- Visual progress (calendars, apps, journals)
- The "don't break the chain" method
- Satisfies need to see progress

## The Inversion (Breaking Bad Habits)

| Law | Build Good Habits | Break Bad Habits |
|-----|-------------------|------------------|
| 1st | Make it obvious | Make it invisible |
| 2nd | Make it attractive | Make it unattractive |
| 3rd | Make it easy | Make it difficult |
| 4th | Make it satisfying | Make it unsatisfying |

## Identity-Based Habits

> "The goal is not to read a book, the goal is to become a reader."

**Three Layers of Behavior Change:**
1. **Outcomes** - What you get (results)
2. **Processes** - What you do (habits)
3. **Identity** - What you believe (self-image)

**Most effective:** Start with identity, not outcomes.

**Identity Shift Examples:**
- "I want to lose weight" → "I am a healthy person"
- "I want to read more" → "I am a reader"
- "I want to run a marathon" → "I am a runner"

## The 1% Rule

> "If you get 1% better each day for one year, you'll end up 37x better."

**Compound Growth:**
- 1% better daily = 37.78x improvement in a year
- 1% worse daily = decline to nearly zero

**Key Insight:** Small habits compound into remarkable results over time.

## Application in CONSIST

### Task Design
- **Obvious**: Clear daily tasks with specific times
- **Attractive**: Connect tasks to user's deeper goals
- **Easy**: Start with 2-minute versions
- **Satisfying**: Immediate celebration, streak tracking

### For Beginners
- Apply Two-Minute Rule aggressively
- Focus on showing up, not performance
- Build identity: "I am someone who [does X]"

### For Struggling Users
- Which law is failing? Diagnose:
  - Forgetting? → Make it more obvious
  - Not motivated? → Make it more attractive
  - Too hard? → Make it easier
  - No reward? → Make it more satisfying

### Coaching Language
- "Let's make this so easy you can't say no"
- "What's the 2-minute version of this?"
- "After you [existing habit], you'll [new habit]"
- "You're becoming someone who [identity]"

# Implementation Intentions — Gollwitzer (1999) and Subsequent Research

## The Core Framework
Peter Gollwitzer's research on implementation intentions addresses one of the most frustrating gaps in behavior change: the intention-action gap. People who have strong intentions to act still fail to act in roughly 40-50% of cases. Implementation intentions solve this by converting vague goals into concrete if-then plans that bypass executive decision-making at the moment of action.

Source: Gollwitzer, P.M. (1999). Implementation intentions: Strong effects of simple plans. American Psychologist, 54(7), 493-503.

Meta-analysis: Gollwitzer, P.M. & Sheeran, P. (2006). Implementation intentions and goal achievement: A meta-analysis of effects and processes. Advances in Experimental Social Psychology, 38, 69-119. Effect size d = 0.65 across 94 independent studies.

## The Structure of an Implementation Intention
Format: "When [situation X] occurs, I will [response Y]."

This differs from a simple goal intention ("I intend to exercise this week") by specifying exactly:
1. When — the triggering cue (time, location, event, emotional state)
2. What — the exact response behavior (not a general intention, but a specific action)

Example transformation:
- Goal intention: "I want to practice coding every day."
- Implementation intention: "When I sit down at my desk after breakfast, I will open the editor and write code for 20 minutes before doing anything else."

## Why It Works — Three Mechanisms

**1. Strategic automaticity**: The if-then link creates a mental association between the situational cue and the intended action. When the cue is encountered, the response is initiated automatically, bypassing the deliberative processes that normally interrupt intentions. Action feels effortless because the decision has already been made.

**2. Heightened cue accessibility**: After forming an implementation intention, people become more likely to notice and remember the triggering situation when it occurs. The cue becomes "perceptually ready" — the brain is primed to detect it. In studies, people with implementation intentions noticed target stimuli 30% faster than those without.

**3. Bypassing competing motivations**: Much intention-action failure is not about forgetting but about competing motivations that win at the moment of decision (checking phone, watching TV). Implementation intentions shield the behavior from these competing responses by replacing the deliberation window with an automatic link.

## Quantified Effect Sizes
Meta-analysis of 94 studies (Gollwitzer & Sheeran, 2006):
- Overall effect: d = 0.65 (medium-large — comparable to 30 IQ points on test performance)
- Trivial goal intentions produce larger if-then benefits (d = 0.84) than already-strong intentions (d = 0.59) — implementation intentions most help where motivation alone is insufficient
- Goal domains where effect is strongest: health behavior change, academic performance, diet, exercise
- Effect holds across populations: students, adults, elderly, people with low self-regulation capacity, ADHD

## The Cue Matters as Much as the Response
Research by Orbell, Hodgkins & Sheeran (1997) showed that the triggering cue must be:
- **Specific and observable**: "When I feel tired" is too vague. "When my alarm goes off at 7am" is concrete.
- **Personally relevant**: The cue must be part of the person's actual daily environment, not hypothetical.
- **Temporally precise**: Situational cues (events, times, places) outperform internal state cues (emotions, urges).

The best cues are part of existing routines — this is why habit stacking (Duhigg, Clear) amplifies implementation intentions. The established behavior is the cue; the new behavior is the response.

## Coping Planning (Sniehotta Extension, 2005)
Extension by Sniehotta, Schwarzer, Scholz & Schüz (2005) adds a second layer: obstacle-specific if-then plans for anticipated barriers.

Format: "If [obstacle Z] occurs, then I will [coping response]."

Example: "If I feel too tired after work to study, I will study for just 10 minutes instead."

The combination of action planning + coping planning increases behavior by approximately 20-30% over action planning alone. In the Consist context: every task should have a micro-session fallback that is activated by the cue "I have less time/energy than expected."

## Application to AI Curriculum and Task Design

### Task-Level Implementation Intentions
Every assigned task should anchor to an environmental cue from the user's own routine. The goal intake process should capture: when does the user typically study? Where? What happens immediately before?

Example output from intake: "You mentioned you study after dinner. Implementation: 'When dinner is done and dishes are cleared, I will open [app] and start today's task — even if only for 10 minutes.'"

### The TimeConstraint Stone and If-Then Fallbacks
For users with TimeConstraint stones, every task needs an explicit micro-session implementation intention as a fallback:
"If I only have 5-10 minutes, I will do [specific micro-version of today's task]."

This converts a potential skip (decision: can I do the full task? No. Skip.) into a partial completion (decision: already made — do the micro version automatically).

### Inconsistency Stone: Never-Miss-Twice Coping Plan
For users with Inconsistency stones, the implementation intention structure:
"If I miss a day, I will do tomorrow's task within 30 minutes of waking, regardless of how I feel."

This prevents the typical two-day gap that precedes full habit abandonment. The coping plan activates automatically rather than requiring motivational recovery.

### Weekly Review Implementation Intentions
Research shows implementation intentions for review sessions are even more effective than for practice sessions because reviews feel less intrinsically motivating. Pre-specifying when/where/how to review closes the most likely drop-off point in a spaced repetition curriculum.

## Evidence Summary for Coaching
- "Having a specific plan (if-then) doubles to triples follow-through vs. vague intentions" (Gollwitzer & Sheeran, 2006)
- "Effect is strongest when motivation alone is insufficient — exactly the condition most learners face mid-curriculum"
- "Adding a backup plan for obstacles increases behavior by 20-30% beyond action planning alone" (Sniehotta et al., 2005)
- "Cue specificity matters: time and place cues outperform emotional state cues"

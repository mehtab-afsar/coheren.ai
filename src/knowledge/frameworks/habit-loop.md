# The Habit Loop
**Source:** Charles Duhigg - The Power of Habit

## Core Model

```
    ┌─────────┐
    │   CUE   │ ← Trigger that initiates behavior
    └────┬────┘
         │
         ▼
    ┌─────────┐
    │ ROUTINE │ ← The behavior itself
    └────┬────┘
         │
         ▼
    ┌─────────┐
    │ REWARD  │ ← The benefit that reinforces the loop
    └─────────┘
```

## The Three Components

### 1. Cue (Trigger)
The signal that tells your brain to go into automatic mode.

**Types of Cues:**
- **Location**: Where you are
- **Time**: When it happens
- **Emotional State**: How you feel
- **Other People**: Who you're with
- **Preceding Action**: What just happened

**Examples:**
- Alarm goes off (time) → morning routine
- Feeling stressed (emotion) → snacking
- After brushing teeth (preceding action) → flossing

### 2. Routine (Behavior)
The actual habit - physical, mental, or emotional.

**Can be:**
- Physical: Running, eating, smoking
- Mental: Thinking patterns, worry loops
- Emotional: Reactions, responses

### 3. Reward (Reinforcement)
The benefit that makes the habit worth remembering.

**Types of Rewards:**
- Physical: Food, rest, pleasure
- Emotional: Pride, relief, excitement
- Social: Approval, connection, status
- Mental: Satisfaction, accomplishment

## The Golden Rule of Habit Change

> "You can't extinguish a bad habit, you can only change it."

**To change a habit:**
1. Keep the same CUE
2. Keep the same REWARD
3. Change the ROUTINE

**Example - Stress eating:**
- Cue: Feeling stressed at 3pm
- Old routine: Eat cookies
- Reward: Distraction, energy boost, pleasure
- New routine: Walk around block (same distraction, energy)

## Keystone Habits

Certain habits have ripple effects that spread to other areas.

**Common Keystone Habits:**
- Exercise → better eating, sleep, productivity
- Making bed → feeling of control, tidiness spreads
- Family dinners → better grades, communication
- Tracking → awareness leads to improvement

**For CONSIST:** Identify if user's goal could be a keystone habit.

## Craving: The Hidden Engine

Between cue and routine lies **craving** - the anticipation of reward.

```
CUE → CRAVING → ROUTINE → REWARD
```

**Key insight:** Habits persist because we crave the reward, not because we need it.

## Application in CONSIST

### Task Design
- Clear cue: "After [anchor], do [task]"
- Defined routine: Specific, actionable task
- Built-in reward: Completion celebration, progress tracking

### Habit Stacking (from Atomic Habits)
```
After [CURRENT HABIT], I will [NEW HABIT]
```
- After I pour my coffee, I will write one sentence
- After I sit at my desk, I will review my goals
- After I brush my teeth, I will meditate for 2 minutes

### Breaking Bad Habits
1. Identify the cue (what triggers it?)
2. Identify the reward (what do you really want?)
3. Find a new routine that delivers the same reward

### For Struggling Users
- "What's happening right before you skip the task?" (identify cue)
- "What would make this feel rewarding?" (enhance reward)
- "Can we make the routine smaller?" (reduce friction)

## Research-Backed Insights

1. **21 days is a myth** - habits take 18-254 days (avg 66 days)
2. **Context matters** - habits are location-dependent
3. **Missing once is ok** - missing twice starts a new habit
4. **Rewards must be immediate** - delayed rewards don't reinforce

## Coaching Language

**For new habits:**
"Let's attach this to something you already do every day."

**For breaking habits:**
"What are you really getting from this habit? Let's find a healthier way to get that."

**For struggling:**
"Habits live in a loop. Let's figure out what's triggering the skip."

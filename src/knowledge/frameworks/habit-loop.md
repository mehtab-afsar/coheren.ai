# The Power of Habit — Charles Duhigg

## The Habit Loop
Every habit follows three stages: CUE → ROUTINE → REWARD.
CUE: The trigger that initiates the behavior. Can be a location, time of day, emotional state, other people, or a preceding action.
ROUTINE: The behavior itself. Can be physical, mental, or emotional.
REWARD: The benefit that reinforces the loop. Can be a pleasure, a relief of craving, a sense of satisfaction, or a social acknowledgment.
Golden Rule of Habit Change: You cannot extinguish a habit — you can only change it. Keep the same cue and reward, and replace only the routine.

## Keystone Habits
Certain habits create ripple effects that transform other areas of life. These are keystone habits.
Exercise is the most powerful keystone habit — it consistently leads to better eating, improved sleep, higher productivity, and greater patience. Making your bed each morning creates a sense of order that cascades into other decisions. Family dinners improve children's academic performance and emotional regulation.
Identifying a user's goal as a keystone habit — one that will unintentionally improve other areas — dramatically increases motivation and long-term adherence.

## The Craving Brain
Habits are driven by neurological cravings. When a cue is encountered, the brain anticipates the reward and creates a craving for it before the routine even begins.
This is why willpower-based change fails: the craving is felt before the behavior is initiated. The routine must be redesigned to satisfy the same underlying craving.
Example: If the cue is "afternoon boredom" and the reward is "social stimulation," a walk to the coffee shop (social) satisfies the same craving as eating junk food (social eating). Change the routine, keep the reward.

## The Role of Belief
Change requires belief. People with strong habits often relapse under stress unless they have a new belief system (a community, a philosophy, an identity) to support the new routine.
This is why groups and social accountability amplify habit change — they provide shared belief that change is possible even when individual motivation is low.

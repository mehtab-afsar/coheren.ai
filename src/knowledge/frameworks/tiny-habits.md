# Tiny Habits — BJ Fogg

## The B=MAP Formula
Behavior = Motivation × Ability × Prompt. All three must converge simultaneously for a behavior to occur.
Motivation and Ability can compensate for each other — a highly motivated person can do hard things; a very easy thing requires little motivation. But if the Prompt is missing, nothing happens regardless of motivation or ability.
The fatal flaw of most habit programs: they focus on motivation (unreliable) instead of Ability (permanent) and Prompt (controllable).

## The Tiny Habit Recipe
Formula: "After I [ANCHOR MOMENT], I will [TINY BEHAVIOR]."
The anchor is an existing behavior that reliably happens. The tiny behavior is scaled to 30 seconds or less so motivation is never required.
Examples: After I start the coffee maker, I will do two push-ups. After I hear my morning alarm, I will put on my workout shoes. After I open my laptop, I will write one sentence.
Key principle: Make the behavior so tiny that you would feel silly not doing it.

## Celebration and the Shine Feeling
Immediate positive emotion after completing a behavior is the most powerful tool for habit formation.
Say "Victory!" or "Yes!", do a small fist pump, feel genuine pride. This creates a positive emotional association and releases dopamine, wiring the habit into the basal ganglia.
Fake celebrations do not work — the brain knows the difference. The goal is to feel the "Shine" — a genuine sense of competence and success, however small.
People change best by feeling good, not by feeling bad. Shame and guilt are the least effective change tools.

## Motivation is Unreliable
Motivation fluctuates wildly with mood, sleep, stress, and circumstances. Designing habits that depend on high motivation guarantees failure on low-motivation days.
The solution is to design habits that work at the lowest level of motivation. Ask: "What is the simplest version of this habit I could do even on my worst day?"
When motivation is high, you can always do more. When it is low, the tiny habit still fires.

## Growing Habits Naturally
Do not force the growth of habits. When a habit feels easy and natural, it will grow on its own.
The metaphor of a garden: plant tiny seeds (tiny habits), water them with celebration, and they will grow without being forced. Forcing growth too fast leads to burnout and abandonment.
Trust the compound growth: a tiny behavior done 100 times creates a deep neural pathway. A large behavior done 5 times does not.
